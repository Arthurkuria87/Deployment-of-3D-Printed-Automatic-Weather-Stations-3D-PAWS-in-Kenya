# Evaluation and Deployment of 3D-Printed Automatic Weather Stations (3D-PAWS) in Kenya with Integrated Data Visualization and Monitoring Systems

## About the Project

This repository contains the research paper and supporting materials associated with the evaluation and deployment of 3D-Printed Automatic Weather Stations (3D-PAWS) in Kenya.
The project focuses on the development, deployment, validation, monitoring and evaluation of locally fabricated 3D-PAWS stations as a means of strengthening and densifying Kenya's meteorological observation network.
The deployed systems integrate meteorological sensors, solar-battery power systems, cellular communication and cloud-based data transmission and visualization infrastructure.

## Abstract
This study presents the deployment and evaluation framework of a 3D-Printed Automatic Weather Station (3D-PAWS) in Kenya. It offers a cost-effective and scalable alternative to conventional Automatic Weather Stations (AWS). Reliable meteorological data is crucial for sectors like agriculture, aviation, and climate monitoring. However, standard AWS systems often face challenges such as high costs, complex maintenance, and limited coverage, particularly in Regional Association I (RA I).
The 3D-PAWS system comprises multiple environmental sensors, including those for temperature, humidity, pressure, rainfall, wind, and solar radiation, all housed within a modular 3D-printed and locally available materials structure. The system runs on a solar-battery setup and uses a cellular-enabled microcontroller to send data in real time to cloud-based platforms. Users can access this data through the CHORDS Portal (https://3d-fewsnet.icdp.ucar.edu). We are also developing a custom dashboard for the Kenya Meteorological Department to facilitate ongoing monitoring, visualization, and management of a network of stations.
A significant aspect of the system is its local fabrication approach. 3D printing, assembly, and initial sensor calibration occur in-house at the Kenya Meteorological Service Authority (KMD), Kenya Workshop. This approach improves system adaptability, lowers deployment costs, and promotes sustainable scaling of weather monitoring infrastructure. with collaboration with our development partners CHC, FewsNet, UCAR, UCSB under the EMNP Program.
This paper outlines the system design, deployment architecture, and data infrastructure of 3D-PAWS in Kenya. We propose a comparative analysis with high-end AWS and manual observations to evaluate performance and reliability, as a foundation for further analysis.


## Objectives

The project aims to:
- Evaluate the performance of 3D-PAWS stations deployed in Kenya.
- Assess the quality and availability of observations generated by the stations.
- Evaluate the potential of 3D-PAWS for densifying the meteorological observation network.
- Document deployment, operation and maintenance experiences.
- Integrate station observations into data transmission, visualization and monitoring systems.
- Support the development of locally appropriate and sustainable weather observation solutions.

## 3D-PAWS System

The deployed 3D-PAWS systems incorporate:
- Meteorological sensors
- Locally fabricated station components
- Solar power systems
- Battery storage
- Cellular communication
- Automated data transmission
- Cloud-based data infrastructure
- Data visualization and monitoring systems

The systems are designed to provide observations that can complement existing meteorological observations and support improved spatial coverage.

## Deployment in Kenya

The project includes 3D-PAWS stations deployed at different locations and host institutions across Kenya.
The deployment network includes stations hosted by meteorological offices, research institutions, educational institutions, airports, agricultural facilities and other partner organizations.
The repository will provide station metadata and supporting information associated with the deployment.

## Data and Validation
The project includes assessment of observations generated by the 3D-PAWS stations.
Evaluation includes comparison and validation of meteorological observations against reference observations where appropriate.
The assessment covers parameters including:

- Temperature
- Relative humidity
- Atmospheric pressure
- Rainfall

The evaluation also considers data availability, completeness, quality control and station performance.
